Where you already are

You are not starting from nothing

Most communities we speak with already have more than they get credit for. This page is not a replacement for any of it.

Programming guidelines

Standards set at the top, with each community free to tailor the calendar to its own residents.

What sits underneath all three is the resident, holding a phone, asking a question nobody hears. That is the gap we fill.

What we are solving

Four things operators tell us

Two are problems today. Two are opportunities you can already see.

Residents worry about AI

They hear a lot about it and are not sure what is safe. Voice-cloning calls and fake emergencies aimed at seniors have made that worry reasonable.

That worry sits in the way of the good it can do them.

The benefit is being left on the table

Companionship, memory keeping, and connection to family are exactly what a good AI assistant can help with. Most residents never get it, because nobody hands it to them.

Safety is always a risk

Seniors meet AI on their own, in their own browser, with no guardrails and nobody to ask.

Tools built for seniors, with guardrails built in and a connection back to the community, give them a safer place to be.

Everyone is out of step

A bulletin board in the dining room and a flyer in the elevator reach some residents and almost no family members.

When the calendar, the residents, the families, and the staff share one schedule, engagement goes up.

The decision usually starts somewhere specific: a fall, a hospital stay, a spouse's death, a caregiver who is worn out. In that moment the adult child is doing the research, often from another state, and often at night. An easy way to see what a parent is actually doing can help them feel better about the choice, and can help decide it.

What your programming team gets

Working today

The basics, handled

  • Events you enter become a calendar, without rebuilding it somewhere else
  • Residents ask the Concierge what is coming up, and get an answer in plain words
  • Reminders reach the people who signed up, by text or email once they turn it on
  • Photos, captions, and stories from an event are kept together
  • A family member can be given access to the same calendar and memories
In build for sponsors

The work that takes the most hours

  • A flyer generated for each event, in your community's style
  • Sign-ups and attendance recorded, per event
  • A plain-language report written for the family, on a schedule you choose
  • An alert when people signed up but did not come
  • Suggestions for new programs, based on what your residents actually attend

Two events can also run at the same hour when they want different people. Today that decision is a guess. With attendance data it is a comparison.

Which programs are filling up across the company, and which ones have stopped pulling people in.

Memory care and legacy

Yesterday's gardening day, saved by last night

A memory is pictures, a story, and a voice, kept together where the family can find it.

One memory, three parts

Photos, the story in the resident's own words, and audio recorded in their voice. The Concierge keeps them as one memory, not three separate files.

A resident can add it to their own albums. A family member can listen to it again years later.

From a pile of pictures to a short film

A set of photos, a story, and audio can be turned into a short movie of the memory. This is being built now.

Later, the same material becomes the record of a life: the story a family returns to, and the one they can read aloud at the end.

For memory care, the value is different. Not every resident can describe their day, but almost every family wants to know it was a good one.

Inside the community

A memory posted once, seen by everyone connected

Familiar to anyone who uses Facebook, with none of the noise, and only the people connected to your community.

The team posts it

Photos and a few lines from the activity, posted to the community after the event.

Residents and families see it

It waits for them on the Spirantix page. They can add it to their own albums, or say they liked it.

The people who missed it catch up

Someone who stayed in their room sees what happened, and knows about the next one.

For the resident, it is a reminder of a good day. For the daughter three states away, it is the answer to "what did Mom do this week?" without a phone call she does not have time for.

A win on three sides

Who benefits, and how

The resident

A better day, more of the time. Programs they actually want, a calendar they can ask about, and their memories kept in their own voice.

The community team

Hours back from flyers and sign-up sheets, and a clear answer on which programs worked instead of a guess.

The organization

Said plainly

What we do not do

  • We do not replace your programming team, your family portal, or your life-story tool. We sit next to them.
  • We do not give clinical advice, and we do not touch care records. Care decisions stay with your clinicians.
  • We do not identify a person from a photograph.
  • We do not train our models on your residents' information.
  • We do not put a resident in front of a chatbot with no guardrails and no connection to the community.
