AI tool highlights

Good places to begin with AI

These general-purpose assistants can explain ideas, help with writing, organize plans, work with images, and answer follow-up questions in a conversational way.

ChatGPT

Use text or voice to ask questions, explore ideas, draft writing, compare options, and get everyday help.

Explore ChatGPT

Claude

Ask questions, work through ideas, simplify complex information, analyze documents, and create written material.

Explore Claude

Gemini

Google’s AI assistant can help with writing, learning, planning, images, and tasks connected to Google services.

Explore Gemini

Microsoft Copilot

An AI assistant for questions, research, writing, and creating content, with additional features inside Microsoft 365 apps.

Explore Copilot

Use AI as a helper, not a final authority

AI tools can make mistakes. Verify important health, legal, financial, and safety information with a qualified professional or an authoritative source, and avoid sharing sensitive personal information.

App highlights

Useful apps with practical benefits

Some use AI directly, while others make books, information, or everyday assistance easier to access.

Be My Eyes

Connect with volunteers or AI-powered visual assistance for help describing objects, reading labels, and completing visual tasks.

Learn about Be My Eyes

Seeing AI

Microsoft’s visual-assistance app uses a phone camera and AI to describe text, people, products, currency, and surroundings.

Learn about Seeing AI

Google Lens

Use a phone camera or an existing image to identify objects, copy or translate text, and search for related information.

Learn about Google Lens

Libby

Borrow ebooks, audiobooks, and magazines using a library card from a participating public library.

Learn about Libby

Supporting resources

Reliable help when accuracy matters

AI is not the right source for every question. These official websites are better starting points for health coverage, aging information, and scam guidance.

National Institute on Aging

Research-based information about healthy aging, health conditions, caregiving, and clinical research.

Visit NIA

Medicare

Official information about Medicare coverage, plans, providers, cards, premiums, and fraud reporting.

Visit Medicare.gov

FTC Consumer Advice

Current guidance for recognizing, avoiding, and reporting scams, including scams aimed at older adults.

Visit FTC Consumer Advice

How we choose

Clear, useful, and worth your time

We favor tools and apps with clear everyday uses, approachable experiences, responsible privacy practices, and genuine value for older adults. A listing is not a guarantee of every feature, answer, product, or service offered by an outside organization.
